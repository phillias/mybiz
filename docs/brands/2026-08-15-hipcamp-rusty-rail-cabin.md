## 1. Brand identity

**Primary name: The Rusty Rail Cabin** — nods to the North Western State Trail (the old rail line) running Petoskey → Alanson → Mackinaw City. Memorable, distinctly Northwoods, tells the "trailhead camp" story.

Alternatives: Crooked Lake Camp Cabin · Hearth & Jackpine · Powers Road Hideout · Burt Lake Bunkhouse.
(Hipcamp allows one property name; set the site name independently, e.g. property "The Rusty Rail Cabin," site "The Cabin (entire cabin).")

**Tagline:** *"Log walls, real fire, dark skies."*
Alternates: "The tent's optional." · "Come for the woods, stay for the fire." · "A camp with a roof."

**Positioning:** For outdoorsy travelers, families, and camp-curious first-timers who want the full Northwoods experience without sleeping on the ground: a hand-built log cabin on a quiet country road minutes from Burt Lake, Crooked Lake, and the Inland Waterway — campfire nights, stargazing, trail days, four full seasons. Unlike a boutique vacation rental it stays rustic, honest, and outdoors-first; unlike a tent site it has real walls, real beds, and a real bathroom.

**Brand story (host voice, ~160 words):**
> This cabin started as a two-year project I built mostly by hand — every log notched, a hundred small arguments with a chainsaw. I'm a builder by trade and a tinkerer by default, so I wired it for modern life while leaving the woods exactly as I found them: quiet, dark at night, and full of stars you forgot existed.
>
> I grew up running the Inland Waterway between Burt Lake and Crooked Lake, and I still think the best day is a slow morning on the porch, a paddle before lunch, and a fire that outlasts the conversation at night. On Hipcamp, I want you to feel like you've been loaned my camp — not checked into a hotel. The cabin gives you walls and a real bed, but the point of the place is outside: the fire, the dark sky, the deer at the tree line, the smell of pine smoke in your coat.

> Come for the woods. Stay for the fire. Leave with a Petoskey stone in your pocket.

**Cross-platform guardrail:** the Airbnb listing positions this same cabin as a comfortable, self-contained rental. The Hipcamp brand must be the outdoor/camping-flavored alternative — always lead with fire pit, woods, stars, and the trail; never "boutique," "luxury," or "getaway retreat."

## 3. Listing description (~700 words, host voice)

**Welcome to camp.** Pull in off Powers Road and the woods take over. The Rusty Rail Cabin is a hand-built log cabin set back on a quiet country road near Alanson — a real camp, with a roof. The fire pit is already set with a stack of dry wood, the Adirondack chairs face the trees, and the only traffic you'll hear all weekend is a passing deer. Bring your boots, your fishing rods, your dog, and your slowest weeknight.

**The cabin.** Inside it's honest Northwoods: log walls, a warm living room, a full kitchen, a real bathroom with hot water, and bedrooms that sleep six (plus a pullout if the crew's big). It's comfortable — not fancy. Think flannel, not chandeliers. The wood stove and furnace keep it toasty, and the porch is the best seat in the house for a morning coffee or an evening listen. The cabin is the shelter; the point of the place is outside.

**Camp life: fire, food, stars.** This is a fire-pit cabin. In the evenings we build it up, you roast whatever you hauled in, and the sky goes properly dark — the Milky Way shows up on clear nights in a way it never does in the city. There's a camp kitchen and grill for cookouts, a picnic table, and quiet. No floodlights, no road noise, no neighbors in sight. Bring marshmallows. Leave your phone in the car if you can.

**Around here: water, trails, four seasons.** You're five to fifteen minutes from the water. Burt Lake and Crooked Lake both have public launches for boats, kayaks, and paddleboards, and the old Alanson swing bridge over the Crooked River is the classic postcard moment. The Inland Waterway — that chain of lakes and rivers running from Crooked Lake to Lake Huron — is one of the best paddling routes in the Midwest, and it starts basically at the end of the road.

The North Western State Trail passes through Alanson on its way from Petoskey to Mackinaw City — perfect for bikes, hiking, and in winter, snowshoes, cross-country skis, and snowmobiles. Downtown Alanson is five minutes for coffee, bait, and a burger; Petoskey's gaslight district and bayfront are a twenty-minute drive. Nub's Nob and Boyne Highlands are about forty minutes for downhill.

Fall is the reason we live here — peak color usually rolls through in early-to-mid October and the whole county lights up. Winter brings real snow, ice fishing on the lakes, and a cabin that's never been cozier. Spring and summer are all water.

**What to know before you book:**
- Cell service is spotty inside the cabin — a bar or two near a window, the porch, or up the drive. That's the point, but plan for it. No Wi-Fi, on purpose.
- Bathroom & water: full bathroom, hot water, drinkable well water. On septic — no wipes or anything but toilet paper.
- Wildlife is real. Black bear country: keep food and trash inside, never leave the cooler out overnight, give deer, turkeys, and raccoons their space.
- Quiet hours 10pm–8am. No amplified music outside; fire out by midnight.
- Fire rules: campfires only in the fire ring. Michigan seasonal open-burn restrictions (especially spring); DNR burn-permit status noted in the welcome message; never burn garbage.
- Pets welcome (max two), on-leash outdoors, cleaned up after, never left alone in the cabin.

**Booking.** Whole-cabin, private booking — you won't share the property. Check-in 3pm, check-out 11am. Driveway parking for up to three vehicles. Door code + full arrival guide sent the day of check-in; host one message away during the stay.

Come be our guest. Bring the dog, the kids, the fishing poles, and an open evening for the fire. — *The Rusty Rail Cabin host*

## 4. Sites & amenities strategy

**5 differentiators:**
1. Quiet rural privacy — country road, wooded setback, no visible neighbors.
2. A real campfire — pre-stocked ring, Adirondack chairs, free first firewood.
3. 5–15 min to the water — Burt Lake, Crooked Lake, the Inland Waterway, the Alanson swing bridge, at a fraction of waterfront prices. *(Verify exact launch distances.)*
4. Stargazing — low light pollution, dark skies, no floodlights.
5. Year-round — most regional cabins close Nov–Apr; winter here is scarce inventory (ski, snowmobile, ice fishing) and a genuine edge.

## 6. Pricing strategy

Position: a real cabin at camp prices — above tent sites ($30–$60/night in northern MI), **25–40% below waterfront cabins/glamping** ($250–$400+). The "rustic but real" sweet spot.

| Season | Weekday | Fri/Sat | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shoulder (Apr–May, Sep) | $155 | $175 | Spring burn-restriction risk |
| Summer peak (Jun–Aug) | $175 | $205 | Anchor season |
| Fall color (early–mid Oct) | $195 | $225 | Sell hard in Sept |
| Winter (Dec–Mar) | $185 | $195 | Ski/snowmobile/cozy; Xmas/New Year = holiday pricing |

- Holiday multiplier 1.5–1.75× (Jul 4, Labor Day, Thanksgiving, Christmas/NYE).
- Min stay: 2 nights summer weekends; 3 on major holidays; 1 night OK on shoulder/winter weekdays.
- Firewood: 2 bundles + kindling included (differentiator); extras $8/bundle.
- Launch promo: first 3 bookings at 10–15% off (or $20–30/night new-host discount) to earn initial reviews.
- **Fees (verify in dashboard — change over time):** hosts pay ~15% commission on private-land listings (12.5% if PMS-connected; was 10% historically); guests pay their own ~10% service fee. A $195/night stay nets roughly ~$166 before taxes. Price with the 15% in mind — listed rate should match what the same stay costs on Airbnb so platforms don't undercut each other.
- Winter strategy: keep 4-season availability on (scarcest inventory in the region); consider a "2-for-1 midweek winter" special to seed cold-season reviews.

## 7. Booking rules & host notes

- **Cancellation:** **Moderate** (full refund minus Hipcamp fees up to 3 days before check-in; none after) as baseline — camp-minded guests plan loosely. Toggle to **Strict** (7 days) for summer peak. 9 tiers exist (Super Flexible → Super Strict); pick per-season.
- **Check-in/out:** 3pm / 11am. Self-check-in with door code sent the morning of arrival (tech-native host = frictionless arrival).
- **Pets:** allowed, max 2, leashed outdoors, picked-up-after, never left alone inside. **$0 pet fee** (keeps the campy feel); revisit if damage appears.
- **Fire rules (seasonal, Michigan):** fire ring only; obey **DNR open-burn restrictions** (typically late March–May + dry red-flag days); check DNR burn-permit status daily in spring and note in welcome message; never burn treated wood or trash; fully douse before bed.
- **Quiet hours:** 10pm–8am; no amplified outdoor music; fire out by midnight.
- **Wildlife:** black bear country — food/trash in the cabin, coolers indoors overnight, leave porch light on at night.
- **Guest instructions:** arrival message with door code + 60-second phone-tour video; in-cabin laminated map (drives to Burt/Crooked launches, Alanson swing bridge, trailheads, groceries); local eats list; fire-ring starter kit by the door.
- **First-guest onboarding:** welcome note 3 days out; door code + directions morning of arrival; check-in text an hour after arrival; in-app review request day after checkout. A hand-built fire starter kit (~$15) buys five-star warmth.
- **Also:** no smoking indoors, no candles on bare wood (battery lanterns provided), no parties/events, max 6 occupants, day visitors by arrangement, septic-safe only.
